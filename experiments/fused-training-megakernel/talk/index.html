One kernel runs the whole training step. This is the map of exactly where — and why — it still loses.

The full forward + backward + grad-zero of a Qwen3-architecture model runs as one persistent CUDA kernel on one H100. It races the strongest normal stack: compile+CUDAGraph+ — torch.compile plus whole-step CUDA-graph replay (the “+” = extra hardening).

Why fuse? A kernel dies with its pipeline: near its end the loads stop and the machine drains; the next kernel restarts from cold — and no kernel can load data for the next one. One persistent kernel can. It can also hand background work to idle SMs. The wins slide draws this; the rest of the deck maps the price. ↓

the honest meter · every win row · the caching caveat
  • Dual-bound meter (bb199ea re-anchor): each megakernel cell is on-path µs .. on-path + overlapped µs against the baseline's serialized wall. The lower bound alone is generous to the megakernel (overlapped span is free); the upper bound alone is generous to the baseline (it charges work that provably hid). Cells that win even at the upper bound are unambiguous.
  • Win rows in words: cross-entropy wins at nano (0.7×) and s1024 (0.8–0.9×); rowops-bwd wins at nano and deep (0.6–0.9×) — exactly the short reduction/elementwise chains where kernel boundaries and extra memory round-trips hurt replay most. dW GEMMs' lower bound is 0 µs at 6 of 10 shapes: the weight-grad work rides entirely off the critical path.
  • embed reads red but is tiny: 7–119 µs absolute at these shapes — the ratio is loud, the microseconds are not.
  • The caching caveat: of the two theory wins, only the boundary/overlap class has cashed out so far. Explicit weight residency (weights parked in smem across ops) measured 0.91× — at 1/8 occupancy the weight bytes already ride the latency window the chain exposes ("only chain-shortening pays", two slides down). L2 does the cross-op weight caching implicitly either way.

what moved long-S attention · packing law
  • The long-S bwd deficit was the C=1 causal-triangle straggler: makespan = the longest tile's serial chain. Banded chunking (split only the long tiles, per-band workspace slots) recovered ~530 µs at S=8192 bwd and ~480 µs fwd via flash-decoding partials + range-limited combine.
  • Post-banding the bwd bands are 77–83% packed (stage-units × per-stage cost / 132 blocks vs actual window) — scheduling knobs can recover ≤10%; the rest is per-stage op cost: dKV ~2.7 µs and dQ ~1.9 µs per 64-row stage ≈ 200 TF/s machine-wide. The honest target is FA4 measured at our shapes: 389 TF/s at S=8192 — the oft-quoted “flash ~500 TF/s” is a machine-filling-shape artifact (at S 2048–4096, B=1, FA4 itself only reaches 137–285 TF/s here). That makes the op gap ~1.9×, of which ~1.4× is the deliberate two-pass recompute: FA4's backward is one-pass.
  • Jul-7 state: chunk retunes, sink ordering, and hop-span levers all measured-refuted at S4096/S8192 — the long-S residual needs an attention body mechanism. Active lanes: one-pass backward under the producer-executor register budget (validated standalone Jul 7 — the old one-pass refutation traced to its atomic drain), cp.reduce bulk drains, and producer-warpgroup softmax offload.
  • Bucket ratios here are trace-share ratios exactly as in the operator-gap report (megakernel on-path µs ÷ baseline active+gap µs).

09 · Why — first principles

One kernel, one resource point

measured law
Only chain-shortening pays

Caching, saving traffic, deeper pipelines, prefetching — all measured slower here. Every real win removed a link from the chain of dependent waits.

measured law
The ops aren't the gap

Run alone, the same code matches the libraries (GEMM 1.03× of cuBLAS, attention ≈ flash). Inside the megakernel the identical instruction pays +30–50%. The environment is the tax.

structural constraint
64K registers, one image

Every op must fit one register/thread shape; libraries pick a tuned shape per kernel. Broken Jul 7: a second build adds dedicated loader warps — and each shape picks its build.

closure ladders · code map · protocol
    • NT lm-head “floor” — broken Jul 7: the round-10 verdict (146 µs vs nvjet 86.8 = 1.66×, identical traffic, closed against every SM-side mechanism and raster order, “residual is DRAM writeback policy”) was refuted from the store side: an SW128 64-column-slab TMA C store with store-side EVICT_FIRST reaches 110.7 µs / 1.21 TB/s standalone (from 0.92 TB/s; load-side EVICT_FIRST is harmful).     Closed, open, and what a win takes

    closed — measured & landed
    Environment taxes

    Fast attention for big heads (1169 → 99 µs) · scheduler starvation at giant shapes (14.2 → 0.3 ms) · per-shape kernel builds with dedicated loader warps · long-sequence work splitting · and the “unbeatable” store floor — beaten Jul 7 by a smarter store path.

    open — the two real residuals
    The two open problems

    Feeding the GEMMs data fast enough (mid-size shapes, ≈1.17×) — and attention backward at long S: we recompute the score triangle twice where flash does it once. That alone is ~1.4× of the ~1.9× gap.

    next lanes, ranked
    The queue

    1 · one-pass attention backward (already proven standalone) · 2 · bulk gradient drains · 3 · loader warps for the giant forward GEMM · 4 · softmax offload to the loader warps.

    The ops were never the gap — the environment was. Standalone, the same code matches the libraries. Close the two open problems and the banked wins flip the sign. Trajectory: s8192 went 2.30× → 1.95× in two days. Nearest crossover: deep, short-sequence models (1.30×).
